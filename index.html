What is a profit margin calculator?

A profit margin calculator compares selling price with direct cost. It shows profit per sale, margin as a percentage of revenue, markup as a percentage of cost, and the price required to reach a target margin.

Margin and markup formulas

Profit equals selling price minus cost. Margin is profit divided by selling price. Markup is profit divided by cost. Because the denominators differ, a 50% margin is not the same as a 50% markup.

Example

An item that costs $40 and sells for $80 produces $40 profit, a 50% margin and a 100% markup before overhead and tax.

Does this include overhead?

No. Enter the complete cost relevant to each sale, or calculate a fuller price with the job cost tool.

What is a sales tax calculator?

A sales tax calculator adds a percentage-based tax to a pre-tax price or works backward from a tax-inclusive total. It is useful for checking a receipt, preparing a quote or estimating the amount a customer will pay.

How to calculate sales tax

  1. Choose Add Tax when you know the subtotal before tax.
  2. Choose Reverse Tax when you know only the tax-inclusive total.
  3. Enter the amount and the exact tax rate for the transaction.
  4. Review the subtotal, tax amount and final total.

Sales tax formula and example

Tax equals the pre-tax price multiplied by the tax rate. At an 8.25% rate, a $100 pre-tax sale has $8.25 in tax and a $108.25 total.

Does Searya choose the correct tax rate?

No. Rates and taxability vary by location and product. Confirm the applicable rate with the relevant authority or a qualified tax professional.

JOB COST SUMMARY

What is job cost calculation?

Job costing combines materials, labor and other direct expenses, then allocates overhead and adds markup. Contractors and service businesses can use the result as a starting point for a customer price instead of guessing from labor alone.

How to calculate a job price

  1. Enter materials and every other direct project expense.
  2. Multiply labor hours by the true hourly labor cost.
  3. Add an overhead percentage for operating expenses.
  4. Apply the desired markup and review the resulting gross margin.

Markup is not margin

A 25% markup on a $1,000 complete cost creates a $1,250 price and a 20% gross margin. Margin divides profit by selling price, while markup divides profit by cost.

What belongs in overhead?

Overhead can include insurance, software, rent, vehicles and administrative costs that support jobs but are not entered as direct line items.

What is an hourly rate calculator?

An hourly rate calculator estimates the rate a freelancer or contractor needs to charge based on income goals, annual business expenses, tax allowance, working weeks and realistic billable capacity.

How the hourly rate is calculated

  1. Add your desired annual take-home income and business expenses.
  2. Include a tax or contingency allowance rather than treating gross revenue as personal income.
  3. Enter working weeks, hours per week and the percentage you can actually bill.
  4. Compare the base rate with the 10% and 20% buffer options.

Why billable percentage matters

Sales, administration, revisions and professional development reduce billable capacity. Dividing the annual revenue target by every working hour therefore tends to underprice client work.

Is the result a guaranteed profitable rate?

No. It is a planning estimate. Compare it with market conditions, project risk and your actual costs before quoting.

What is a break-even calculator?

A break-even calculator estimates how many units a business must sell before contribution from sales covers fixed costs. It also shows the revenue associated with that threshold.

How to calculate break-even units

  1. Enter fixed costs for the period being analyzed.
  2. Enter the selling price and variable cost per unit.
  3. Calculate the contribution margin per unit.
  4. Use the rounded whole-unit target for practical planning.

Break-even formula and example

Break-even units equal fixed costs divided by selling price minus variable cost. With $5,000 in fixed costs, a $100 price and $40 variable cost, the contribution is $60 and the exact break-even point is 83.34 units, so the operational target is 84 units.

What if variable cost is higher than the selling price?

There is no positive contribution to cover fixed costs, so the current price and cost structure cannot reach break-even.
